# Traffic collision options (team17/collision-design, 2026-10-21)

This is a design proposal only. Nothing in the game has been changed. The owner chooses an option, then a build team
implements it behind a workspace attribute so it can be switched off.

## สรุปภาษาไทย (สำหรับเจ้าของเกม)

ตอนนี้รถจราจร (traffic) เป็น "กล่องแข็งที่มองไม่เห็น" ซึ่งเกมย้ายตำแหน่งแบบวาร์ปทุกเฟรมและไม่มีความเร็วทางฟิสิกส์ เมื่อรถผู้เล่นชนที่ 150 studs/s
จึงเหมือนชนกำแพง รถเด้งกลับ หมุนแบบสุ่ม และบางครั้งพลิกคว่ำ (เทสต์ล่าสุดคว่ำ up = -0.75) เมื่อคว่ำแล้วรถไม่ตั้งตัวเอง ต้องกด R เอง
และยังไม่มีระบบช่วยเมื่อรถติดกำแพง ต้นไม้ หรือหลุมข้างถนน เราเสนอ 4 ทางเลือก:
**A** คงฟิสิกส์เดิม แต่เพิ่มระบบตั้งรถอัตโนมัติและกู้รถที่ติด
**B** ระบบชนแบบอาร์เคด (ทีมแนะนำ) กล่องรถจราจรเลิกชนทางฟิสิกส์ สคริปต์ตรวจการชนเองแล้วลดความเร็ว (ชนเบา −20%)
หรือทำให้รถหมุนและคุมยาก (ชนแรง: ความเร็วหาย 45–70%, หมุน 45–120°, คุมได้น้อยลง 0.6–1.2 วินาที) และ **รถจราจรทำให้รถพลิกคว่ำไม่ได้เลย**
**C** ฟิสิกส์จริง แต่มีตัวกันรถเอียงเกินกำหนด บวกบทลงโทษความเร็วด้วยสคริปต์
**D** ระหว่างแข่ง รถจราจรเป็นผี (ขับทะลุได้) ส่วนขับเล่นอิสระและงานแท็กซี่ใช้แบบ B
ทุกทางเลือกใช้ "ชุดกู้รถ" ร่วมกัน: ตั้งรถอัตโนมัติเมื่อคว่ำนาน 1.5 วินาที, ปุ่ม R มีคูลดาวน์,
ตรวจจับรถติดแล้วพากลับถนนที่ใกล้ที่สุดโดยหันตามทิศเส้นทาง และใช้ข้ามเช็คพอยต์ไม่ได้
ทีมแนะนำ **B + ชุดกู้รถ** และเตรียม D ไว้เป็นสวิตช์ เผื่อการแข่งบนถนนพึ่งดวงมากเกินไป แต่ผู้ตัดสินใจคือเจ้าของเกม
คำถามที่ต้องการคำตอบอยู่ท้ายเอกสาร (ข้อ 8)

## Plain-language summary

- **What happens today.** Every traffic car is one hidden solid box. The game moves it by jumping it to a new spot
  every frame, so to the physics engine it is a wall with no speed of its own. Hitting it at 150 is like hitting a
  wall. The car bounces back, spins unpredictably and can roll over. Once it is upside down, nothing turns it back
  and the player has to press R. A car stuck on a palm trunk, pole, building corner or road-edge drop gets no help
  except R, which only lifts the car 6 studs where it is.
- **What the owner asked for:** a light hit loses some speed, a heavy hit spins the car or costs control, a flipped
  car can get back, and nobody stays stuck in the scenery.
- **Four options:**
  - **A. Keep the physics and add a safety net.** Crashes stay as they are now, flips included. The car turns
    itself back over after 1.5 s, and a stuck car is put back on the road. This is the smallest change and the least
    "arcade".
  - **B. Arcade hits (team's recommendation).** Traffic stops being a physical wall. The game detects the touch and
    applies a fixed, tuned result. A light hit costs 20 % of your speed. A heavy hit costs 45–70 %, spins the car
    45–120° and weakens the steering for 0.6–1.2 s. Traffic can never flip you, and the result is the same on every
    PC and phone.
  - **C. Physics, but no rolling.** Traffic stays a real wall, but a stabiliser stops the car tilting past about
    25°, and a scripted speed penalty is added on top. It feels more "real" than B but is less predictable.
  - **D. Ghost traffic in races, B everywhere else.** During a race, nearby traffic fades and you drive through it.
    Outside races it behaves as in B.
- **All options share the same recovery kit:** automatic flip-back, an R key with a cooldown, stuck detection, and
  a "rescue" that puts the car on the nearest road facing the right way. None of these can skip a checkpoint.
- **Recommendation (from the team; the owner decides):** B plus the recovery kit, built in two steps with the
  recovery kit first. The kit also fixes flips at road edges, which have nothing to do with traffic. Every piece has
  an off switch.

## 1. How collisions work today

### 1.1 The traffic car (client side)

File: `roblox/src/StarterPlayer/StarterPlayerScripts/PattayaTraffic.client.luau` (304 lines).

| Thing | Value / code |
|---|---|
| Where it runs | Every client runs its own copy. Each car's position is a pure function of `workspace:GetServerTimeNow()` and the route data in `ReplicatedStorage.PattayaTraffic` (36 loops, 464 cars, generated by `tools/mapgen/traffic.py`). It sends nothing over the network. |
| Speeds | 38–55 studs/s per loop (`speed_studs_s` in `tools/mapgen/config/*.json`), one car per 420 studs of loop. |
| Models | Pooled, at most 70 models (`MAX_MODELS`), and only within 650 studs of the camera focus (`RADIUS`). |
| Collider | One `Part` named `Body` per car (`makeCar`, line ~118): **Anchored = true, CanCollide = true, CanQuery = false, CanTouch = false**, with SmoothPlastic default physical properties. All visual parts (the mesh vehicle from `TrafficVehicles.build`, or the box-car pieces) are welded, massless and non-colliding. When a mesh is used, the Body is invisible (`Transparency = 1`). |
| Collider sizes (W × H × L) | kind 0 sedan/taxi 6.6 × 2.4 × 14; kind 1 songthaew 7 × 2.6 × 16; kind 2 van 7.2 × 6 × 16 (`SHAPES`, line 78). The bottom is 1.4 studs above the road (`Lift = 1.4 + h/2`). |
| Movement | `workspace:BulkMoveTo(roots, cframes, FireCFrameChanged)` every Heartbeat (line 295). The box is anchored and teleported, so its physics velocity is **zero**. The solver treats it as a static wall that is somewhere new each frame. |
| Raycasts | `CanQuery = false`, so the player's wheel raycasts never land on traffic. |

**Ghost rule** (lines 262–273). The check runs in the traffic car's own frame (forward = −Z). The traffic car ghosts
for 1.5 s when all of these hold:

- my car is ahead of it in its lane (`rel.Z` between 0 and −(halfLen + 16));
- my car is within `halfWid + 4` sideways;
- my speed along its direction is between −10 and (traffic speed − 5).

Ghosting sets `CanCollide = false` and gives every part `LocalTransparencyModifier = 0.6`. A traffic car therefore
never shoves a stopped or slower player from behind. Every other contact is a real physical crash: head-on, side, and
**me rear-ending it at speed**. When the ghost ends, the box turns solid again even if it still overlaps my car, which
can give a sudden push-out.

**Near miss** (lines 274–290). A near miss needs all of these:

- the car goes from one end of a traffic car to the other with a side gap between 0 and `NEAR_GAP` = 7 studs;
- it never overlaps the traffic car (`lateral <= 0` cancels it);
- the traffic car is not ghosted;
- the player is doing more than `NEAR_SPEED` = 70 studs/s;
- at most one near miss per 0.6 s.

The client then fires `NearMiss`. The server (`PattayaJobsServer.server.luau` lines 220–237) only checks three things:
the player is in a car, the speed it sees is at least 60, and at least 0.6 s has passed since the last one. It pays
nitro `6 + 2 × combo` through `NitroBonus` (the combo grows up to 10 within 4 s, ×2 during events), and a +5 ฿ tip
during a job. Touching a traffic car (overlapping it) loses that near miss and nothing else.

### 1.2 The player car

Server build: `roblox/src/ServerScriptService/RaceCarServer.server.luau`.

- One collider: `Chassis`, 7 × 1.6 × 15 studs, with `CustomPhysicalProperties(0.7, 0, 0, 100, 1)`. That means density
  0.7, friction 0 with weight 100 (so the zero dominates: no ground or wall friction) and elasticity 0 with weight 1.
  Mass ≈ 0.7 × 168 = **118**. The body mesh, wheels and seat are massless; the seated character adds a little.
- The body mesh, wheels and seat do not collide. The chassis centre rides about 2.8 studs above the road
  (`CHASSIS_UP` in RaceSessionService), so it spans about road +2.0 to +3.6. A sedan traffic box spans road +1.4 to
  +3.8, the same height band, so the contact is the chassis edge against a box face.
- The network owner is the driver (`SetNetworkOwner(plr)`, line 261), so all car physics runs on the driver's
  client. The server holds parked cars with a spring and a parking brake.
- Combined bounce against a traffic box: elasticity is the weighted average of 0 (chassis) and 0.5 (SmoothPlastic),
  about 0.25. That matches the earlier measured head-on test, 57 → −14 studs/s (RACING_ROUTE_DESIGN.md "Tested").

Controller: `roblox/src/StarterPlayer/StarterPlayerScripts/CarController.client.luau` (447 lines).

- Raycast suspension at 4 mounts, cast along **−chassis up** (`step`, line 201). `grounded` is the number of wheels
  whose ray hit something.
- `grounded >= 2` (line 244): scripted drive and brake impulse, a grip impulse that kills sideways speed (grip 9/s,
  drift 1.4/s), and a yaw-rate controller that sets **only the yaw part** of the angular velocity. Pitch and roll are
  left to the springs, which are underdamped (damping ratio 0.35).
- `grounded < 2` (line 291): "airborne". Pitch and roll angular velocity are damped at `airUpright` = 3 per second.
  That is weak: after 0.33 s about 37 % of the roll rate is still there.
- Top speeds are 146–182 studs/s and nitro 200–236 (CarSpecs; hard ceiling 245).

### 1.3 What flips the car

No logging covers this yet. This is the team's reading of the code and of the leader's test, where the car hit a
traffic car at about 150 and ended with up vector −0.75:

1. **The box has no speed as far as physics knows.** Rear-ending a 45 studs/s traffic car at 150 is resolved as
   hitting a still wall at 150, not as a 105 closing speed. With 0.25 bounce, the car goes from 150 to about −37 in
   one frame.
2. **Off-centre contact.** Hitting a corner of the box puts the impulse far from the chassis' centre of mass. That
   spins the car on all three axes (yaw, pitch and roll), not just yaw.
3. **Nothing on the ground resists roll fast enough.** On the ground the controller only manages yaw, and pitch and
   roll only meet the soft springs. Once two wheels lift, the airborne damping (3/s) is too slow to stop a roll
   started by a corner hit at 150 studs/s.
4. **Penetration pops.** The box is teleported every frame, and a ghost that ends while overlapping turns solid
   inside the car. The solver pushes the two apart with a large impulse in whatever direction separates them fastest.
5. **Upside down means it stays upside down.** Once the car is past about 90°, the wheel raycasts point at the sky,
   so `grounded` stays 0. The only correction left is the weak airborne damping, which slows rotation but never turns
   the car back over.

The car also flips at road-edge drops for reasons that have nothing to do with traffic (QUALITY_AUDIT_2026-10-20:
Sukhumvit outer lane, the Soi 8 hole on Beach Road). The recovery kit below is needed for those as well.

### 1.5 What a crash does to results today

| Mode | Effect of hitting traffic |
|---|---|
| Free roam | Lost speed and maybe a flip. You also lose the near miss for that car. Nothing else. |
| Taxi / delivery job (`PattayaJobsServer`) | The server measures ground speed from position at 10 Hz. One **bump** is a drop of more than `BUMP_DROP` = 55 studs/s within 0.35 s that stays low for two samples, at most once per second. Full braking loses 45 in that window, so braking alone is never a bump. Each bump cuts the fare by 12 % (taxi) or 15 % (delivery), capped at 60 % in total. Near misses add a +5 ฿ tip each. A heavy hit at job speed (70–85) stops the car and is counted; a glancing scrape usually is not. |
| Loop race, Street Sprint, Street Challenge, Road Fight, Night Run, Survival (`PattayaRaceServer`, `PattayaSprintServer`, `PattayaStreetRace`) | Only the time lost; no penalty and no damage. Survival eliminates the slowest 20 % each round, so one crash there can knock you out. A flip the player cannot fix quickly can mean a DNF (time limits are 120–180 s). |
| AI racers (bots in Challenge, Road Fight, events) | Bots are anchored, non-colliding models that the server moves on rails (`PattayaStreetRace` `botStep`). **They never meet traffic** and never lose time to it, but humans do. |
| Circuit races / Time Attack / Ranked (`RaceSessionService`) | There is no traffic on circuits. Ranked covers only public CircuitRace and is off by default, so traffic has **no ranked effect today**. Player-vs-player contact in CircuitRace and TrackDay is physical; FreePractice and TimeAttack use the `CircuitGhost` collision group. |
| Night Run "ram traffic for nitro" (RayCity Pokju) | Not built. Events only double the near-miss nitro. |

### 1.6 Multiplayer facts that matter for any option

- Each player collides only with **their own client's** copy of the traffic. The copies agree because they are all
  computed from the server clock, but another player's car is drawn 0.1–0.2 s late (15–30 studs at 150). On your
  screen, another driver can seem to pass through a traffic car or crash into "nothing".
- The server can also work out where every traffic car is at any moment, because the route data is in
  ReplicatedStorage and the position formula only depends on the clock. That makes it possible to sanity-check hits
  and near misses, and to give bots traffic, with some tolerance for lag.
- The client owns its car, so **no collision rule is a security boundary**. A cheater can already make traffic
  non-solid (`TK.ghostTraffic` does exactly that). The real server guards are the 260 studs/s teleport checks, the
  rate limits and the checkpoint order.

## 2. Shared recovery kit (used by every option)

The owner asked for flip recovery and anti-stuck whichever way hits end up feeling. These rules are the same in A–D,
and each option says if it changes them.

### 2.1 Auto-upright (client, CarController)

- **Trigger:** either of these:
  - tilt more than 60° (`chassis.CFrame.UpVector.Y < 0.5`) **and** ground speed below 15 studs/s, held for **1.5 s**;
  - upside down (`UpVector.Y < 0`) for **1.0 s** at any speed.

  It never triggers while the chassis is anchored (race grid, server reset) or in the first 0.5 s after entering the
  car.
- **Action:** over 0.35 s, turn the car wheels-down about its own position and lift it 2.5 studs. The heading is the
  flattened current look direction. If that points straight up or down, it uses the direction of travel instead, and
  failing that the direction of the road under the car. Angular velocity goes to zero and at most 20 % of the
  horizontal velocity is kept. A small "RECOVERED" toast shows.
- **Cost:** about 2–2.5 s in total from the flip to driving again. No other penalty.

### 2.2 Manual R (client, CarController `onReset`)

- R / Y / touch R stays "flip in place". It never moves you along the road, so it can never skip a checkpoint.
- Add a **2 s cooldown**, and only allow R when the tilt is over 30° or the speed is under 10 studs/s. It should not
  work as a free emergency brake or lane hop at speed.
- Lift 3 studs instead of 6, which stops hopping over low walls, and check that the space is free (overlap query)
  before placing the car. If it is blocked, fall through to the rescue in 2.3.
- Circuits keep their server **T** reset unchanged (last gate, lap invalid, 3 s cooldown).

### 2.3 Stuck detection and rescue

**Detection (client):** any of these:

- **Stuck:** for **4 s**, throttle or reverse is held (|throttle| > 0.5) and the car has moved less than 6 studs in
  total. This is measured on position, not velocity.
- **Off the road:** the wheel raycasts hit no road material for 6 s while the car is slower than 20 studs/s. Driving
  fast on a beach or grass is allowed.
- **Off the map:** the car falls more than 30 studs below the world's lowest road height.

What the player sees:

- At 4 s, a prompt: "Stuck? Hold R to rescue". It needs a 1 s hold, so it is never pressed by accident.
- At 8 s with no change of input, an automatic rescue. Whether this step exists at all is **owner decision 5**.

**Rescue (server, new remote `RequestRescue`):**

- **Rate limit:** one rescue per **10 s**. The car's speed as the server sees it must be below 25 studs/s, and the
  player must be seated in their car.
- **Free roam / job:** the car goes to the nearest road edge from `PattayaRoute.nearestEdge`, within 300 studs. It
  lands in the left-hand lane (traffic drives on the left). The heading is the edge direction closest to the car's old
  heading; during a job, the one closest to the GPS route direction.
- **Street races (loop race, sprint, challenge, Road Fight, events):** the car goes to the point on the **race
  route** nearest to it, clamped to lie between the last checkpoint passed and 25 studs **before** the next one. The
  heading is the route direction. A rescue can therefore never put you past a checkpoint or further along than you
  were.
- **Circuits:** the existing T reset (last gate, lap invalid).
- **Placement** works like `RaceSessionService.placeChassis`:
  - anchor the chassis, `PivotTo` the new spot and zero the velocity;
  - unanchor after **1.0 s** in races (0.5 s elsewhere) and hand network ownership back;
  - for 2 s afterwards, ghost traffic on that client (the server cannot see client traffic, so this stops a car being
    dropped into one), and ghost the car against other players with a collision group.
- **Server checks:** the race and job servers must be told this was a server move, because their teleport checks
  measure distance over time. A `RescueAt` timestamp that those servers read is enough: a few lines per server, like
  `Tracker:moved` on circuits.
- **Costs:** in races, the 1 s hold plus restarting from 0 makes a rescue always slower than driving. In a job, a
  rescue is not a bump (no fare cut), but the clock keeps running.

### 2.4 Rollback

These are workspace attributes, read live through their changed signals. The defaults keep today's behaviour.

- `AutoUpright` (bool, default false until the owner accepts it)
- `AntiStuck` (bool)
- `RescueAuto` (bool): the automatic rescue at 8 s
- `ResetCooldown` (number, seconds; 0 = today's unlimited R)

## 3. The options

The numbers below are starting values for tuning, not measurements. **Closing speed `vc`** is how fast my car moves
into the traffic car along the contact direction, counting the traffic car's own speed (known from the route).
Rear-ending a 45 studs/s car at 150 gives vc ≈ 105. A shallow side-swipe gives a small vc. A head-on at 150 gives
about 195.

### Option A: keep physical boxes, add the recovery kit

**Idea:** hits feel the same as now, and section 2 is added. Two physics tweaks could come later, but the owner said
no physics changes yet: giving the box its real velocity (so the closing speed is right) and setting its elasticity
to 0 (no bounce-back).

| Hit | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Light (scrape, vc < 30) | Physics: usually 5–25 % of the speed is lost, with a small yaw; sometimes more on a corner. |
| Heavy (vc ≥ 30) | Physics: often a near stop or a bounce back (150 → about −37), a random spin, and **flips are still possible** (as in the leader test). |
| Flip recovery | Auto-upright after 1.0–1.5 s (2.1), and R with a cooldown. |
| Control loss | Whatever the physics gives; there is no scripted window. |

- **Implementation:**
  - CarController: +80–110 lines (auto-upright, R rules, stuck detection, rescue request).
  - New `ServerScriptService/CarRescueServer.server.luau`, ~140 lines: the remote, rate limit, `PattayaRoute` snap,
    and the race-route clamp through a small API on `PattayaStreetRace` and the Sprint and Race servers.
  - 3–6 lines in each of PattayaJobsServer, PattayaSprintServer, PattayaRaceServer and PattayaStreetRace to honour
    `RescueAt`.
  - The traffic file is unchanged.
- **Authority / exploits:** upright and stuck detection run on the client. That is harmless, because they can only
  help your own car. The rescue runs on the server with a cooldown and the checkpoint clamp. A cheater can bypass the
  client-side R cooldown, but R never moves you forward.
- **Racing balance:** unchanged, and still random. One heavy hit costs 2–5 s (stop, regain speed, maybe a flip and a
  2.5 s upright). Sprints only last 10–25 s, so one crash decides a race.
  - Bots still never hit traffic.
  - Job fares and near misses work as today.
  - Ranked: no effect, because there is no traffic on circuits.
- **Multiplayer:** the same desync as today. Other players see you crash into nothing or pass through traffic.
- **Mobile:** auto-upright and auto-rescue help most here, because the R button is small. Physics contact at 30 FPS
  penetrates deeper, so the pops are more violent than on PC.
- **Risk:** the lowest code risk, but it does not deliver "heavy hit = spin, not flip".

### Option B: arcade impulse model (no physical traffic)

**Idea:** the traffic Body stays as the size reference but is **`CanCollide = false` always**. PattayaTraffic already
computes my car's position in each traffic car's frame every frame. It adds three things on top:

- a 2D overlap test of my chassis rectangle against the traffic rectangle. The test is swept from last frame's
  position, so a 30 FPS client cannot skip through a car;
- on first contact, a call to CarController with `{vc, side, frontal angle}`, and CarController applies a scripted
  result;
- after the hit, the traffic car ghosts for 1.5 s (no double hit) and gets a small client-only visual shove: 1–2 studs
  sideways, easing back over 1 s.

| Tier (by closing speed vc) | Speed lost | Yaw kick | Control loss | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scrape** (vc < 15, or glancing < 15°) | 8 % | 0–5° away from the traffic car | none | Cancels that near miss. Light camera nudge. |
| **Light** (15 ≤ vc < 45) | 20 % | 8–15° away | 0.25 s with steering at 70 % | Spark/scrape sound. |
| **Heavy** (vc ≥ 45) | 45 % at vc 45, rising to 70 % at vc ≥ 130 | 45° at vc 45, rising to 120° at vc ≥ 130. The direction depends on which side of the car was hit, and a rear-quarter hit spins more. | 0.6 s at vc 45, rising to 1.2 s. Steering drops to 30 % and grip drops to drift grip, so the car slides. Throttle stays. | Short camera shake, vibration on gamepad/phone. |
| **Head-on** (vc ≥ 90 and the cars face each other within 30°) | 85 % (near stop), then a bounce back at 10 studs/s | 0–20° | 1.0 s | The biggest penalty, but still no roll. |

- **Never roll:** while a control-loss window is open, pitch and roll angular velocity are zeroed every frame (yaw
  stays free). The car is also pushed out of the traffic rectangle along the contact direction, by the overlap plus
  0.5 studs. A traffic hit can never flip the car. Scenery hits (walls, palm trunks, poles) stay physical and rely on
  the recovery kit.
- **Estimated time cost at 150:**
  - light: about 0.05–0.1 s;
  - heavy: about 1–2 s (regaining 70–100 studs/s at 60–74 studs/s², plus the spin);
  - head-on: about 2.5–3 s.

  The cost is predictable, so a skilled driver can judge the risk.
- **Flip recovery / anti-stuck:** section 2 unchanged. It is still needed for road edges and scenery.
- **Implementation:**
  - `PattayaTraffic.client.luau`, +50–70 lines. The collider is never solid (`setGhost` only keeps the fade). The
    overlap/sweep test goes inside the existing per-car loop, which already has `rel`, `halfWid`, `halfLen` and the
    traffic speed. Plus hit tiering, the ghost after a hit and the visual shove. `lateral <= 0` already cancels near
    misses.
  - `CarController.client.luau`, +60–80 lines for the impact: an `applyImpact` function, a control-loss timer that
    `step` reads to scale steering and grip, and the pitch/roll zeroing. The recovery kit (section 2) adds another
    80–110 lines.
  - The two LocalScripts talk through a client-only `BindableEvent`, for example `PlayerScripts.CarImpact`.
  - Jobs: a heavy hit at job speed loses 70 × 45 % = 32 studs/s, which is **below** the server's 55 studs/s bump
    threshold, so it would stop counting. The fix is a client→server `TrafficHit` remote for heavy and head-on hits
    only, rate-limited to 1 per s. The server checks that the car was moving at 40 or more, and can optionally check
    that a traffic car was within 40 studs at that server time, then counts it as a bump. About 25 lines in
    PattayaJobsServer. The speed-drop detector stays for walls.
  - The rescue server is the same as in A.
- **Authority / exploits:** hit detection runs on the client, like all car physics today. A cheater could skip hits,
  or skip sending `TrafficHit`. That is no worse than today, since `TK.ghostTraffic` shows traffic can already be
  ignored. Later, the server can sanity-check hits and near misses against the deterministic traffic positions. Hits
  only ever cost speed and money, so they add nothing that can be farmed.
- **Racing balance:**
  - The time lost per hit is fixed and moderate. A crash hurts, but rarely decides a race on its own (unless it is a
    head-on).
  - Near-miss economy: unchanged (same rule; an overlap pays nothing).
  - Job fares: the same bump count as today once the `TrafficHit` report is added. Light hits do not cut the fare.
  - Bots still never meet traffic. Phase 2: the server computes traffic positions along each bot's path and applies
    the same table (bots slow down and lose speed), so humans and bots face the same traffic.
  - Ranked: no effect today. If street races ever become ranked, B is fair enough, because every client computes the
    same traffic and uses the same penalty table.
- **Multiplayer:** better than today. The car's owner decides the hit, and the result replicates as normal motion, so
  other players see a clean spin rather than a physics bounce. They still see it about 0.15 s late, so the spin may
  start slightly before the cars visibly touch. That is acceptable for an arcade game.
- **Mobile:**
  - The rules are the same everywhere; thanks to the swept test, 30 FPS gives the same tiers as PC.
  - Steering at 30 % during control loss is easier on touch than fighting a physics spin.
  - Vibration through `HapticService` where the device supports it, and a setting to turn camera shake off.
- **Risk:** a medium amount of code, all behind the attribute, and easy to tune by changing numbers.

### Option C: hybrid, physical but roll-limited, plus a scripted penalty

**Idea:** keep `CanCollide = true`, and add a stabiliser to the player chassis: an `AlignOrientation` with
`AlignType = PrimaryAxisParallel`. It keeps the chassis up axis close to the ground normal (taken from the wheel
raycasts, or world up when they miss), and yaw stays free.

- It is switched on only when the tilt exceeds 20°, and for 1.0 s after any contact.
- Its torque is capped so ramps and kerbs still feel right.
- When a traffic contact is detected, using the same overlap test as B for detection only, the client adds a scripted
  penalty on top of the physics.

| Hit | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Light (vc < 45) | Physics bounce or slide (usually 5–25 % lost), plus a scripted top-up so the loss is at least 15 %. Roll stays below 25°. |
| Heavy (vc ≥ 45) | Physics spin (fairly random, 30–180°), plus a scripted loss so the total is at least 45 %. Roll stays below 25°, with 0.5–1.0 s at 50 % steering. A head-on is still a near stop with a bounce (0.25 elasticity) unless the box elasticity is set to 0. |
| Flip recovery | Flips become rare thanks to the stabiliser; auto-upright (2.1) is the backup. |

- **Implementation:**
  - CarController: +40–60 lines (stabiliser setup in `enter`, rules for switching it on and off, torque cap tuning),
    ~30 lines for the penalty top-up, plus the recovery kit.
  - RaceCarServer: +10 lines (the attachment and the AlignOrientation on the chassis, disabled by default).
  - PattayaTraffic: +30 lines (contact detection, and optionally the box velocity and elasticity).
  - Jobs: as today. Physical heavy hits at job speed still drop the speed by more than 55 in most cases, but this
    needs checking.
- **Authority / exploits:** as in A.
- **Racing balance:** better than A, but less predictable than B, because the outcome depends on the contact point,
  the frame rate and the timing. Bots, near misses and ranked are as in A.
- **Multiplayer:** the same physics bounce artefacts as today, minus the flips.
- **Mobile:** physics contact at 30 FPS still penetrates deeper, which means bigger random spins. The stabiliser
  torque must be tuned on a phone, not only on PC.
- **Risk:** this changes how the car feels everywhere (jumps, kerbs, road edges, drifts), because the stabiliser acts
  on every tilt. It needs the most testing. It also "fixes" the road-edge flips, which hides map defects that the QA
  probes should catch.

### Option D: soft ghosting in races, B in free roam and jobs

**Idea:** a policy on top of B. While the player is in a street race (`RaceState == "racing"`, `SprintActive`, or a
challenge or event race), traffic within 80 studs fades to 50 % and never collides: no hit and no penalty. Outside
races (free roam, jobs) the B table applies.

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| In a race | You drive through traffic, with no speed loss and no spin. Passing within 7 studs **without** overlapping still pays a near miss; passing through does not. |
| Free roam / job | Exactly as B. |
| Flip / stuck | Section 2. |

- **Implementation:** B, plus about 15 lines in PattayaTraffic (read the race attributes and switch to always-ghost),
  plus a per-mode list so the owner can choose which modes ghost. That list is a `TrafficGhostModes` attribute, for
  example `"sprint,challenge,events"`.
- **Racing balance:** the fairest option for races. There is no luck involved, humans and bots are equal without new
  bot code, and races have no multiplayer desync. The cost is that races lose the RayCity skill of dodging traffic.
  Near misses become the only interaction with traffic in a race, and players could farm them by weaving. The
  existing 0.6 s rate limit and the combo cap of 10 should be kept.
- **Multiplayer / ranked:** no traffic desync in races at all, which makes it the best fit if street races ever
  become ranked.
- **Mobile:** the easiest to play in races.
- **Risk:** small on top of B. The feel risk is that traffic in races may look fake when you drive through it.

## 4. Implementation notes common to all options

- **Rollback switch:** `workspace:SetAttribute("CollisionModel", ...)` with these values:
  - `"legacy"`: today's behaviour, and the default until the owner accepts a change;
  - `"physical"`: A;
  - `"arcade"`: B;
  - `"hybrid"`: C.

  On top of that, `TrafficGhostModes` (a string; empty = off) controls D, plus the recovery-kit attributes in 2.4.
  The scripts read them live, so the leader can switch modes on a running server.
- **Tuning table:** keep the owner's numbers in one shared module, for example `ReplicatedStorage.CollisionTuning`.
  The server (jobs, bot traffic, hit sanity checks) and the clients then use the same values.
- **Scenery** (buildings, walls, palm trunks, poles, terrain) stays physical in every option and is handled by the
  recovery kit. A soft "wall slide" (scripted deflection along walls) could be a separate option later; it is not
  proposed now.
- **Player vs player** contact in street modes stays physical in every option; it is out of scope here. Circuits
  keep their ghost and collision modes.
- **Order of work:**
  1. The recovery kit, behind `AutoUpright` / `AntiStuck`. It does not depend on the collision choice, and it also
     fixes the road-edge flips.
  2. The chosen collision model, behind `CollisionModel`.
  3. The jobs `TrafficHit` report.
  4. Bots meeting traffic (B/C).
  5. Server sanity checks of hits and near misses against the deterministic traffic.

## 7. Recommendation (team17 recommendation; the owner decides)

The team recommends **Option B plus the shared recovery kit**, built in this order:

1. the recovery kit, which is useful whatever is chosen and also covers the road-edge flips;
2. the B hit model, behind `CollisionModel = "arcade"`;
3. the job `TrafficHit` report;
4. bots meeting traffic.

D's `TrafficGhostModes` stays ready as a switch for any race mode that playtests show depends too much on luck.
Survival is the first candidate, since one crash there can eliminate you.

Why B:

- It is the only option that delivers exactly what the owner described: a light hit costs speed, a heavy hit causes a
  spin or loss of control, and traffic never flips the car.
- It behaves the same on every device and at every frame rate.
- It is tuned with a handful of numbers.
- It does not change how the car feels on jumps, kerbs and drifts.

## 8. Owner decisions needed

1. Collision model: A, B, C or D (the team recommends B).
2. Hit numbers: accept the B table (light −20 %; heavy −45 to −70 % with a 45–120° spin and 0.6–1.2 s of control loss;
   head-on −85 %), or give your own.
3. Should a traffic hit ever be able to flip the car? (Team: never.)
4. Auto-upright: automatic after 1.5 s (team), or only through the R button?
5. Stuck rescue: a prompt at 4 s only, or an automatic rescue at 8 s as well?
6. R key: is a 2 s cooldown with "only when tilted or slow" OK? Should R be allowed during street races? (It never
   moves you forward.)
7. Which race modes keep solid traffic (B) and which ghost it (D): loop race, Street Sprint, Street Challenge, Road
   Fight, Night Run, Survival.
8. Jobs: should light hits cut the fare, or only heavy hits? (Team: only heavy, which is roughly what today's
   detector does.)
9. Night Run "ram traffic for nitro" like RayCity Pokju: yes or no? (Not built today.)
10. Bots: should AI racers also be slowed by traffic for fairness (phase 2)?
11. Camera shake and phone vibration on heavy hits: on by default, with a setting to turn them off?
